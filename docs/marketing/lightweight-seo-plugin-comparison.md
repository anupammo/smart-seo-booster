Most "best SEO plugin" posts compare feature checklists. That's the wrong axis. Every plugin here outputs title tags, meta descriptions, Open Graph tags, and a sitemap. On the things that actually move rankings, they are functionally equivalent.

What separates them is **how much plugin you're agreeing to run**, and **what they want from you later**.

## The two real questions

**1. Do you want a coach or an appliance?**

Yoast and Rank Math are coaches — traffic lights, content analysis, prompts, dashboards. Genuinely valuable if you're learning SEO. Noise if you already know what a meta description is.

Slim SEO and The SEO Framework are appliances. Install, and correct tags appear. There's almost nothing to configure, because they've made the sensible decisions for you.

**2. What's the upsell pressure?**

This is where free plugins diverge most sharply, and it's rarely discussed honestly. Some are complete products that happen to be free. Others are functional demos with persistent nudges toward a paid tier.

### And the one I wrote

**Smart SEO Booster** — new, under 10 installs, no ratings yet.

I built it to sit between the two camps: the automation of Slim SEO with the visual feedback of Rank Math, without the upsell layer. Everything is free, including redirects and bulk editing. It also does something the others currently don't — an `llms.txt` file and AI-crawler controls for how AI assistants handle your content.

**Reasons not to install it:** it has effectively no user base, which means bugs I haven't found yet, and no track record. For an SEO plugin — where a bad update can quietly cost you traffic — that's a real risk, and "the author says it's good" is not a substitute for thousands of sites having run it. If you're managing a client site or anything revenue-critical, pick one of the six above. I'd rather you use Slim SEO or The SEO Framework and be happy than install mine and get bitten.

## What I'd actually recommend

- **New to SEO, want guidance:** Rank Math. The free tier is the most generous of the coaches, and the guidance is real.
- **Established site, know what you're doing, want speed:** The SEO Framework. Fastest path to correct output with no ongoing attention.
- **Absolute minimum intervention:** Slim SEO. Install it and stop thinking about SEO.
- **Care about privacy / hate admin ads:** SEOPress.
- **Already on Yoast and it works:** stay. Migrating a working setup for marginal gains is a bad trade. "Which SEO plugin" matters far less than publishing things worth ranking.

## The thing nobody says

Switching SEO plugins will not fix your rankings.

Every plugin here produces near-identical output for a correctly configured site. If your traffic is flat, the cause is almost certainly content, backlinks, or site speed — not your choice of plugin. The honest reason to switch is that your current one annoys you, slows your admin, or paywalls something you need. Those are fine reasons. "This one will rank me higher" is not, from any of us.
